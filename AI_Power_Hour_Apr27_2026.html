🛡️ BREACH Mythos — "Too Dangerous" AI Gets Stolen

An AI that found 271 Firefox bugs in days (what would take human teams months) was accessed by an unauthorized group. Anthropic restricted it because it's "too capable" at hacking — but containment failed anyway. CISA (US cybersecurity agency) wasn't even told about the rollout.
Why this is dangerous — a real analogy: Imagine someone built a master key that opens every lock in every house on your street. They decided it was "too dangerous" and locked it in a safe. But someone stole the safe. Now that master key is out there. That's Mythos — except instead of house locks, it opens software vulnerabilities in browsers, operating systems, and infrastructure used by billions.

What Mythos can do: It doesn't just find bugs — it finds exploitable bugs. Mozilla said it's "every bit as capable as elite human researchers." In the wrong hands, it could discover zero-day attacks in banking apps, hospital systems, or power grids faster than any human team could patch them.

For your security team: Review what AI tools have access to your systems. If frontier models get compromised, what's your exposure? This is the AI version of "supply chain attack" — except the payload is an AI that actively searches for weaknesses.

⚖️ LEGAL Sullivan & Cromwell AI Hallucination

A $2,000/hour law firm (Trump's own lawyers) filed court papers with AI-hallucinated case citations — 3 full pages of legal cases that don't exist. The firm had to publicly apologize. If the best lawyers in the world can't catch AI making things up, who can?
What actually happened: Sullivan & Cromwell used AI to research legal precedents. The AI invented case names, court dates, and rulings that sound completely real but never happened. They submitted these fake cases to a federal court.

💡 Real-life example everyone can relate to: Imagine you're writing a school report and ask AI "give me 5 sources that support my argument." The AI gives you 5 perfect-sounding book titles, authors, and page numbers — except none of the books exist. You turn in your report, your teacher Googles the sources, finds nothing, and you fail. That's exactly what happened to one of America's most prestigious law firms, except the consequences are federal sanctions, not a failing grade.

This isn't rare — it's the #1 AI risk today. AI models "hallucinate" (confidently state false information) in 3-15% of outputs depending on the task. The more specific the claim (dates, names, citations), the higher the risk.

Action item: Never trust AI-generated facts without verification. Treat AI like a brilliant intern who sometimes makes things up with total confidence.
